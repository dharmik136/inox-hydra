Privacy policy: Inox Hydra Bridge

Last updated: 25 October 2026. Applies to Inox Hydra Bridge version 2.7.0.

Inox Hydra Bridge is a browser extension that connects the LinkedIn pages you open to Inox Hydra, a program that runs on your own computer. The extension has no server. Neither does Inox Hydra. There is no account to create, no sign-in to the developer, and no analytics or crash reporting of any kind.

The developer of this extension receives nothing from it. Not your posts, not your leads, not usage statistics, not an installation count beyond what the browser stores publish to every developer.

In one paragraph

The extension reads the LinkedIn pages you have open in your own browser and sends what it reads to Inox Hydra on your own computer, at http://127.0.0.1:8000, which is an address that exists only inside your machine. That is the only address the extension ever sends anything to. The data stays in a database file on your disk, under your control, and you can delete it by deleting the file.

What the extension reads

From the LinkedIn pages you open

The extension reads the LinkedIn pages you open. A history import is the one place where it also acts on them, and it does that only after you press "Import your history" in Setup, and only on your own posts, comments or reactions. During that import it clicks LinkedIn's own "Show more results" control and scrolls the page, so it can read further back than what was already on screen, and when you asked for all three kinds it moves the tab itself from one of your own activity pages to the next. So it does open a page for you, during that import and nowhere else, and its click is what makes LinkedIn's own page fetch more. The extension itself never fetches from LinkedIn, during an import or at any other time.

It does one other thing to a LinkedIn page, in the other direction, and it is described under LinkedIn's composer below: when you ask for a draft to be placed in the composer, it opens the composer and types. It never posts, comments, reacts, messages or connects.

From other people's activity on your posts (content.js): the names, profile addresses, headlines and comment or reaction details of people who commented on or reacted to a post, as those appear on the page, together with which post they engaged on. This is how the leads list in Inox Hydra is built.

From your post analytics pages (content.js): the impression, reaction, comment and repost figures LinkedIn shows you for your own posts.

From your own profile and activity pages (own_pages.js), and only yours: your profile fields, experience, education and skills; the contact details on your own contact-info overlay (email, phone, birthday, websites) if you open it; the posts you wrote; the comments you left; the posts you reacted to. The extension establishes that a profile is yours from signs only the owner of a profile sees, or from LinkedIn itself resolving /in/me/ to it, and then from the profile name you confirmed in Inox Hydra. A profile that is not yours is never read by this file.

From LinkedIn's composer (content.js): nothing is read. Text travels the other way, when you ask Inox Hydra to place a draft you wrote into the composer: if the composer is not already open, the extension clicks LinkedIn's own "Start a post" control to open it, and then types your draft into it. Nothing is posted for you; you still press LinkedIn's own Post button.

Only if you switch "Read LinkedIn's network responses" on

This setting is in the extension's popup and is off when you install the extension. It stays off until you switch it on, and switching it off stops the behaviour at once.

While it is on, two further things happen:

  1. A script runs inside LinkedIn's own page context (voyager_interceptor.js) and wraps the page's fetch function so that it can copy the JSON of responses LinkedIn's own application has already downloaded for the page you are looking at: feed updates, profiles, creator analytics, social actions and comments. The figures in those responses are exact rather than rounded, which is the reason the option exists. The script never issues a request of its own and never modifies a request or a response.
  2. The extension notes which of LinkedIn's internal data requests a page made (background.js, using chrome.webRequest.onCompleted): the address, the HTTP status and the method. Not the contents. This log is what Inox Hydra uses to tell you when LinkedIn has changed something and a reading has stopped working.

While the setting is off, the first script is not registered in your browser at all, and Inox Hydra refuses the two data paths that feed from it.

Wrapping the page's fetch function is something LinkedIn may regard as automation. That is written beside the switch in the extension, in those words, before you turn it on.

Cookies

The extension reads cookies through the browser's cookies API in exactly two situations.

The local token, every time it talks to Inox Hydra. Inox Hydra issues an inox_studio_token cookie to 127.0.0.1 and localhost so that only a browser you have paired can reach your own data. The extension reads that cookie and sends it back as a request header. It never leaves your machine.

Your LinkedIn session, only when you press "Sync LinkedIn Session". When you press that button in the popup, and at no other time, the extension reads your li_at and JSESSIONID cookies for linkedin.com and stores them in Inox Hydra on your computer. They are used for one feature: handing a finished post to LinkedIn's own scheduler ("Send to LinkedIn"). Nothing that reads LinkedIn needs them, because reading is done on the pages you open. Inox Hydra's Setup screen shows whether a session is held and has a control to delete it. There is no timer: an earlier version copied the session every fifteen minutes, and that was removed.

The extension also keeps a short one-way digest of your li_at cookie (a truncated SHA-256 hash, never the cookie itself) so that it can notice when you sign out or sign in as somebody else and stop filing readings under the previous account.

What is stored, and where

In the browser, under the extension's own storage: whether network reading is on; the profile name of the LinkedIn account this browser is signed in to; that member's LinkedIn id; the cookie digest described above; and the captures Inox Hydra did not take, so that your own activity is not lost. A capture is held when Inox Hydra is not running, when its database is busy and it asks to be tried again, and when it refuses this browser because the browser has not been paired with it yet. Those held captures contain the same engagement details described above, including the name, profile address, headline and comment of a person who engaged with your post. They are sent to Inox Hydra on this computer at the next successful contact and then removed, at most 200 are held, the oldest going first, and removing the extension removes all of it. Inox Hydra's Today screen says how many are waiting, once the extension has reported.

On your computer, in Inox Hydra's own folder (its location is shown in the app, and is set by INOX_HYDRA_HOME): a SQLite database file holding your posts, drafts, queue, post readings, leads and their engagement, your comments and reactions, any connections you imported from your own LinkedIn data archive, daily analytics, logs and settings; your media files; the network readings log, as a separate file; and the daily backups folder.

What you ask of it, and what it works out for you, in that same database: the text of the asks you make of the studio and choose to keep (the topics, audience and things to avoid you type at Setup; an ask typed into the From your record section; an instruction you tick "remember this ask" on in the Composer, and only then; an idea sent from Telegram; and a card you ask for more of), nothing typed anywhere else; the marks you put on the daily cards (keep, skip and hide, and the studio's own used and posted marks when a draft comes from a card); the daily batch of cards itself, one dated row per card with the rows it was built from; a monthly note you may write about whether the cards helped; and, only for the files you ticked at import, what your own LinkedIn archive says you saved, followed, voted on, reposted and searched for, each row with the file it came from and the date it was imported. Asks and marks are kept until you retire or unhide them on the section's "What the studio knows" page; dated batches are kept for the number of days you set there, 365 by default, except a batch a mark or a draft still refers to. None of it is in the diagnostics bundle or the access log, and none of it leaves this computer unless you have entered an AI provider key and the section's model stage is on, in which case the AI row below says exactly what goes.

If you keep more than one creator profile, each profile is a separate folder with its own database, under profiles/ inside that same folder, and nothing of one profile's data is in another's. The vault key, the logs and the browser profiles belong to the machine and are held once, outside them.

These database files are not encrypted. Keep your operating system's disk encryption on. Anyone with access to your user account on this computer can read them.

How long it is kept, and how to remove it. Inox Hydra's "Storage and privacy" panel shows how much space each kind of data takes, lets you set retention for post readings, reactions and the network readings log, lets you review and forget people who have gone quiet, and lets you compact the database so that removed rows no longer occupy the file. Forgetting a person removes their lead record, every engagement of theirs the studio read, their stage history and any enrichment. If you also imported your LinkedIn connections, their row there is separate and stays until you remove it; so does a record of your own commenting or reacting on their posts. Everything is on your disk, so you can also simply delete the folder. There is nothing of yours anywhere else to request or to delete.

Third parties

In its default configuration, the extension contacts nobody. Every outbound destination below is reached by Inox Hydra on your computer, not by the extension, and only at the moment you use the feature that needs it. The daily section's model stage is such a moment: it runs when you open From your record for the first time each day, never on a timer and never while you are away, and only once you have entered a key.

Destination When it is reached
www.linkedin.comChecking your stored session is still valid, syncing your profile, and "Send to LinkedIn", which hands a post you wrote to LinkedIn's own scheduler. Off by default, the same as update checking below: nothing reaches LinkedIn until you set INOX_ALLOW_LINKEDIN_EGRESS=1, and the first time you use Send to LinkedIn for a given creator profile, it also asks you to acknowledge, separately from the per-send confirmation, that this writes to LinkedIn's private scheduling API, outside its published terms. It is asked once for each creator profile rather than once for the machine, because each profile is a different LinkedIn account and the account that carries the risk is the one that answers for it.
api.openai.com, api.anthropic.com, api.groq.com, generativelanguage.googleapis.comDrafting and rewriting help, if you entered your own API key for that provider. Your prompt and the text you are working on go to the provider you chose, under your own account with them, governed by their terms, not ours. The From your record section's model stage, once a day when you open the section and only with a key entered, sends a redacted set of your own rows to the same provider: the first 300 characters of your own posts and their ids, the studio's own word for how each did (above usual, usual, below usual, not measured), your topic labels, the asks you kept and your voice profile. It never sends figures, other people's names or comments, your connections or anybody's score, and a name, an address or a phone number inside your own text is replaced before it leaves. It spends at most the daily ceiling you set on the section, 10 cents by default, and the model may return only ids of rows it was sent, labels from a fixed list and short phrases; anything else, and any phrase carrying a number, is dropped before it is shown.
image.pollinations.aiGenerating an image, if the image provider is left at its default. This is a free public service run by a third party, and it is the default, so the first time you press Generate without having chosen a provider, your image prompt goes there. Choose another provider, a local one, or Off in the Prompt tab if you would rather it did not.
api.stability.ai, api.replicate.com, fal.run, api.together.xyzGenerating an image, if you chose that provider and entered your own key.
A local image generator (AUTOMATIC1111, Forge or ComfyUI) or a local model through OllamaIf you chose it. These stay on your machine or your network.
A program you connected as a grounding source (MCP)Only if you add one yourself in Brand Studio. The studio starts that program and reads what it hands back, which you can preview; no writing control in the studio draws on it yet. What that program itself does on the network is its own, outside the studio's view, and INOX_NO_EGRESS cannot stop it.
github.com, raw.githubusercontent.comChecking whether a newer version has been released, and fetching the Inox library when you press Fetch on From your record's What the studio knows page. Update checking is off by default, and nothing in the interface turns it on: you enable it yourself from the command line (python -m studio.cli update enable). The library is one file of forms, patterns and examples the Inox Hydra team wrote; the request for it carries nothing of yours, only its address, a name for the channel and the tag of the copy you already hold, and the file is kept only if its signature matches the key the studio ships.
api.telegram.orgAn optional inbound bridge, inactive unless you set a TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN yourself.

If you configure an AI or image provider, that provider's own privacy policy governs what it does with what you send it. We do not see it, do not proxy it, and hold no key of yours anywhere but on your own disk.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or transfer your data to anyone. There is no recipient: the data does not reach us.
  • We do not use or transfer your data for any purpose unrelated to the extension's single purpose.
  • We do not use or transfer your data to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes.
  • We do not serve advertising, build advertising profiles, or include any third-party tracker, analytics library or tag.
  • We do not load or execute any code fetched at runtime. Everything the extension runs is in the uploaded package.

Children

Inox Hydra Bridge is a tool for people who use LinkedIn professionally and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If what the extension reads or where anything is sent changes, this page is updated and the "Last updated" date above changes with it. The version of the extension a change applies to is named.
