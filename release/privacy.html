Privacy policy: Inox Hydra Bridge

Last updated: 29 October 2026. Applies to Inox Hydra Bridge version 2.12.0.

Inox Hydra Bridge is a browser extension that connects the LinkedIn pages you open to Inox Hydra, a program that runs on your own computer. The extension has no server. Neither does Inox Hydra. There is no account to create, no sign-in to the developer, and no analytics or crash reporting of any kind.

The developer of this extension receives nothing from it. Not your posts, not your leads, not usage statistics, not an installation count beyond what the browser stores publish to every developer.

In one paragraph

The extension reads the LinkedIn pages you have open in your own browser and sends what it reads to Inox Hydra on your own computer, at http://127.0.0.1:8000, which is an address that exists only inside your machine. That is the only address the extension ever sends anything to. The data stays in a database file on your disk, under your control, and you can delete it by deleting the file.

What the extension reads

From the LinkedIn pages you open

The extension reads the LinkedIn pages you open. A history import is the one place where it also acts on them, and it does that only after you press "Import your history" in Setup, and only on your own posts, comments or reactions. During that import it clicks LinkedIn's own "Show more results" control and scrolls the page, so it can read further back than what was already on screen, and when you asked for all three kinds it moves the tab itself from one of your own activity pages to the next. So it does open a page for you, during that import and nowhere else, and its click is what makes LinkedIn's own page fetch more. The extension itself never fetches from LinkedIn, during an import or at any other time.

It does one other thing to a LinkedIn page, in the other direction, and it is described under LinkedIn's composer below: when you ask for a draft to be placed in the composer, it opens the composer and types. It never posts, comments, reacts, messages or connects.

When the extension is installed or updated, it starts its readers in the LinkedIn tabs you already have open, as a reload of each tab would, so that no tab has to be reloaded by hand. They read what is described here and nothing more, and nothing is added to the page.

From other people's activity on your posts (content.js): the names, profile addresses, headlines and comment or reaction details of people who commented on or reacted to a post, as those appear on the page, together with which post they engaged on. This is how the leads list in Inox Hydra is built.

From your post analytics pages (own_pages.js), for your own recorded posts only: the figures LinkedIn shows you there, which are impressions, members reached, reactions, comments, reposts, saves and sends, and, where the page shows them, followers gained from the post, profile viewers from the post, link visits, article views, video views, the share of impressions from inside and from outside your network, and the viewer demographics for the post, which are LinkedIn's aggregate percentages (such as the share of viewers with a job title or in a location), never names, and for a video post its watch time and average watch time. Each reading is kept with the post's age and the day it was read.

From your creator analytics page (content.js), for the account you confirmed in Inox Hydra only: the figures LinkedIn shows you there over the range the page is set to, which are impressions, members reached, reactions, comments, reposts, saves and sends, the share of impressions from inside and from outside your network, and on LinkedIn's earlier layout your follower count. A reading over the past 24 hours is kept as that day; a reading over any other range, or one whose range the page does not name, is kept as a reading over that range on the day it was read, never as a day.

From your own account and content analytics pages (own_insights.js), and only yours: on your analytics dashboard, your profile views and search appearances, each with the window the page names (such as the past 90 days), and your new followers by day from the followers graph; the profile viewer count on "Who viewed your profile" and the count on your search appearances page, with their windows, and never a viewer's or a searcher's name, headline or company; and on your own articles' and newsletters' analytics pages, their article views, impressions, members reached, reactions, comments, reposts, subscribers, email sends and email open rate. From your own Activity tab it also keeps that you reposted a post, when, and the repost's own figures where they can be told apart, never the original's author, words or figures, and when one of your posts was first seen marked edited.

From your own profile and activity pages (own_pages.js), and only yours: your profile fields, experience, education and skills; the contact details on your own contact-info overlay (email, phone, birthday, websites) if you open it; the posts you wrote; the comments you left; the posts you reacted to. The extension establishes that a profile is yours from signs only the owner of a profile sees, or from LinkedIn itself resolving /in/me/ to it, and then from the profile name you confirmed in Inox Hydra. A profile that is not yours is never read by this file.

From LinkedIn's composer (content.js): nothing is read. Text travels the other way, when you ask Inox Hydra to place a draft you wrote into the composer: if the composer is not already open, the extension clicks LinkedIn's own "Start a post" control to open it, and then types your draft into it. Nothing is posted for you; you still press LinkedIn's own Post button.

Network reading, retired in 2.10.2

Earlier versions had a setting in the extension's popup, "Read LinkedIn's network responses", off when the extension was installed. While it was on, a script inside LinkedIn's own page context (voyager_interceptor.js) wrapped the page's fetch function to copy the figures LinkedIn's own application had already downloaded for your own posts and your follower count, and the extension noted the addresses of LinkedIn's internal data requests, through the browser's webRequest permission.

LinkedIn's site no longer sends the data it read: its 2026 web app makes none of those requests, and loads its data in a form the extension cannot read. So network reading was retired in Inox Hydra Bridge 2.10.2. The setting, the script and the observer are gone, and the extension no longer asks for the webRequest permission. No part of the extension wraps the page's fetch or XMLHttpRequest, or watches LinkedIn's requests. When it is installed or updated, and when the browser starts, it removes the script an earlier version had registered in your browser, and the stored setting. A LinkedIn tab that was open with the setting on keeps the old wrapper, which sends nothing anywhere, until you reload or close it.

What network reading kept while it ran stays in Inox Hydra's database until you remove it: the figures of your own posts, marked as read by network reading, your follower count, and the record of its runs. Inox Hydra keeps nothing new from it: an extension that has not been updated is answered, and what it sends is not written.

Cookies

The extension reads cookies through the browser's cookies API in exactly two situations.

The local token, every time it talks to Inox Hydra. Inox Hydra issues an inox_studio_token cookie to 127.0.0.1 and localhost so that only a browser you have paired can reach your own data. The extension reads that cookie and sends it back as a request header. It never leaves your machine.

Your LinkedIn session, only when you press "Sync LinkedIn Session". When you press that button in the popup, and at no other time, the extension reads your li_at and JSESSIONID cookies for linkedin.com and stores them in Inox Hydra on your computer. They are used for one feature: handing a finished post to LinkedIn's own scheduler ("Send to LinkedIn"). Nothing that reads LinkedIn needs them, because reading is done on the pages you open. Inox Hydra's Setup screen shows whether a session is held and has a control to delete it. There is no timer: an earlier version copied the session every fifteen minutes, and that was removed.

The extension also keeps a short one-way digest of your li_at cookie (a truncated SHA-256 hash, never the cookie itself) so that it can notice when you sign out or sign in as somebody else and stop filing readings under the previous account.

What is stored, and where

In the browser, under the extension's own storage: the profile name of the LinkedIn account this browser is signed in to; that member's LinkedIn id; the cookie digest described above; a random identifier the extension makes the first time it runs in this browser, which says nothing about you or the browser and lets Inox Hydra tell two browsers apart; a one-way digest (SHA-256) of each LinkedIn account your creator profiles in Inox Hydra have confirmed, never the account's name, so that a capture held for one of those accounts is sent as soon as it has a home; the version number the extension found in its own files at its last look, whether it could read them, and when; a record of the last capture, saying what kind of thing it was, when, what Inox Hydra answered and which tab it came from, never who; this session's count of captures per tab, for the toolbar badge; and, until the browser closes, the last notice that a part of the capture could not read a page (naming the part, the kind of page and the fields) and the last notice a LinkedIn page had for you, in Inox Hydra's own words, which the side panel shows instead of drawing it into LinkedIn's page; a list of the LinkedIn tabs open, each with the kind of page it shows, whether it is in view and the version of the extension's readers it carries, and which tabs could not be given new readers after an update; and a one-way digest (SHA-256) of each capture it sent, never the capture itself, which it uses for ten seconds so that the same capture read in two tabs is sent once; and the captures Inox Hydra did not take, so that your own activity is not lost. A capture is held when Inox Hydra is not running, when its database is busy and it asks to be tried again, and when it refuses this browser because the browser has not been paired with it yet. A capture is also held when it was read while this browser is signed in to a LinkedIn account that none of your creator profiles has confirmed: Inox Hydra writes it into no profile, and the extension keeps it, marked with that account's profile name, until a profile confirms the account. Those held captures contain the same engagement details described above, including the name, profile address, headline and comment of a person who engaged with your post. They are sent to Inox Hydra on this computer at the next successful contact (one held for an account, once a profile has confirmed it) and then removed, at most 200 are held, the oldest going first, and removing the extension removes all of it. Inox Hydra's Today screen says how many are waiting, once the extension has reported, and its Health screen says how many are held for which account.

On your computer, in Inox Hydra's own folder (its location is shown in the app, and is set by INOX_HYDRA_HOME): a SQLite database file holding your posts, drafts, queue, post readings, leads and their engagement, your comments and reactions, any connections you imported from your own LinkedIn data archive, daily analytics, everything you sent your Telegram bot (your collection: each item's words, its kind, when it arrived and what it became), logs and settings; your media files, among them the photos, voice notes, documents and videos you sent your bot, kept as they were sent; the extension readings log, as a separate file, which holds a copy of each analytics reading the extension sends and, from before network reading was retired, what it noted of LinkedIn's requests; and the daily backups folder.

What you ask of it, and what it works out for you, in that same database: the text of the asks you make of the studio and choose to keep (the topics, audience and things to avoid you type at Setup; an ask typed into the From your record section; an instruction you tick "remember this ask" on in the Composer, and only then; an idea sent from Telegram; a card you ask for more of; and a search from your archive, only when you ticked that file at import and it shares one of your recurring topics), nothing typed anywhere else; the things to remember you tell the studio about yourself, your audience, your offers and dates, typed in Brand Studio under Writing or sent to your bot with /remember, with how often each went with a writing request, and, on a draft the studio wrote, which items and which things to remember it was written from; the marks you put on the daily cards (keep, skip and hide, and the studio's own used and posted marks when a draft comes from a card); the daily batch of cards itself, one dated row per card with the rows it was built from; a monthly note you may write about whether the cards helped; and, only for the files you ticked at import, what your own LinkedIn archive says you saved, followed, voted on, reposted and searched for, each row with the file it came from and the date it was imported. Nothing removes an ask or a mark on its own: an ask goes when you remove it on the section's "What the studio knows" page, a keep or a hide when you take it back, and all of them when you delete what the studio holds about you. Dated batches are kept for the number of days you set on that page, 365 by default, except a batch a mark or a draft still refers to. None of it is in the diagnostics bundle or the access log, and none of it leaves this computer unless you have entered an AI provider key and the section's model stage is on, in which case the AI row below says exactly what goes.

If you import LinkedIn's analytics export, the .xlsx file LinkedIn writes when you press Export on your analytics page, Inox Hydra reads it on your computer and keeps the figures it recognises in that database, each marked as read from LinkedIn's export: your figures by day, your recorded posts' figures, and the demographics of your audience and of who engaged with your posts. Those demographics are shares, such as the percentage of your audience with one job title or in one location, kept with the last day the export covers; they name no one, and nothing else in those sheets is kept. A copy you chose by hand is kept only until it is imported or set aside, or for a day, and nothing in the file is sent anywhere.

If you keep more than one creator profile, each profile is a separate folder with its own database, under profiles/ inside that same folder, and nothing of one profile's data is in another's. The vault key, the logs and the browser profiles belong to the machine and are held once, outside them. Each capture the extension sends names the LinkedIn account its browser is signed in to, which is your own, and Inox Hydra files it in the profile that confirmed that account; a capture for an account no profile has confirmed is written into none of them and stays held in the browser, as described above.

The bridge's own record, in the database of the creator profile that is open, or, for a reader's run and a history import, of the profile that confirmed the account the browser is signed in to: for each browser the extension runs in, at most one check-in a minute, saying when, the extension's version and the version found in its files, whether it could read them and when it looked, which browser it is (as the browser names itself, such as Microsoft Edge or Brave), the random identifier described above, the profile name of the LinkedIn account it is signed in to, how many LinkedIn pages of each kind it had open, how many captures it held for which account, how many tabs still carried an older copy of its readers, and whether it reloads itself; with each run of a reader, which browser ran it; and with a history import, which browser and tab ran it, so that two tabs never both scroll. Every capture also says which tab, window and page it was read in, and Inox Hydra keeps no more of that than is listed here. Check-ins and runs are kept for seven days, except each reader's latest run and its latest run that worked. Inox Hydra also remembers, in memory only and for ten minutes, a one-way digest of each capture it took, so that the same capture sent twice is answered "already seen" and not written again; none of it is written to disk.

These database files are not encrypted. Keep your operating system's disk encryption on. Anyone with access to your user account on this computer can read them.

How long it is kept, and how to remove it. Inox Hydra's "Storage and privacy" panel shows how much space each kind of data takes, lets you set retention for post readings, reactions and the extension readings log, lets you review and forget people who have gone quiet, and lets you compact the database so that removed rows no longer occupy the file. Forgetting a person removes their lead record, every engagement of theirs the studio read, their stage history and any enrichment. If you also imported your LinkedIn connections, their row there is separate and stays until you remove it; so does a record of your own commenting or reacting on their posts. Everything is on your disk, so you can also simply delete the folder. There is nothing of yours anywhere else to request or to delete.

Third parties

In its default configuration, the extension contacts nobody. Every outbound destination below is reached by Inox Hydra on your computer, not by the extension, and only at the moment you use the feature that needs it. The daily section's model stage is such a moment: it runs when you open From your record for the first time each day, never on a timer and never while you are away, and only once you have entered a key.

With that on, Send me the whole post (Send me the posts when several are due), under a draft or a due post, sends that post's whole text, as Inox Hydra holds it, when you press it, so you can copy it into the LinkedIn app; nothing is posted for you. With that on too, a draft the bot wrote carries your rewrite buttons (Shorter, New opening and Another angle unless you change them in Brand Studio under Writing): a press sends your provider the draft as it is, the items it was written from, your things to remember as you chose, your writing preferences and that button's own words (see the AI row above), and the bot answers with the rewritten draft's first line; the text a rewrite replaced is kept on the draft, the ten newest, so you can put it back. /remember keeps a sentence of yours as a thing to remember, as does Remember this under a note or an idea you sent, with Undo, and /forget removes the one whose words match yours, never guessing between several; each is answered with a fixed sentence. /recall answers with how many items in your collection hold a word, and their first lines only with The bot may send me what it writes from my collection on. With that on, Send me an idea from my collection each morning, off until you turn it on, sends one unused item's first line with an angle in words from what worked in your own posts, never a figure, with Write it up and Not today. Some of the bot's messages carry buttons you press in Telegram: Undo under what you sent, which takes it back from your studio while it is untouched there, and Remind me in an hour and Done under a due post, where Done only clears the reminder and marks nothing as posted. A press comes back to Inox Hydra through Telegram as a message does, carrying an id that names nothing and none of your words, and is answered with one more fixed sentence; the message is then edited to say what became of it. What each button does is kept in Inox Hydra's settings, for the newest 100 messages that carry buttons, and deleted with everything else. A photo (or several sent together, kept as one item), a voice note, a document or a video you send is downloaded from Telegram as it is (Telegram's getFile, then the file, each counted here like the bot's other requests) and kept with your collection in your media folder: never read, so no text is taken from a photo and a voice note is never transcribed, and nothing of either is sent anywhere; its caption is kept as your words. A file over 20 MB is not downloaded. A document's own name is kept with it, and a file Inox Hydra cannot show is kept as a download only. Add to your media library copies a photo, a PDF or an MP4 or WebM video into your media library, where it stays like anything you upload, even after the item is removed. A link you hid behind words in a message or a caption is kept in the item's words, after them, as if you had pasted it. Everything you send lands in your collection in Inox Hydra, and nothing is written from it until you ask: text, and a message you forward, as your note (a forward's text is kept, and never the forward's origin: who or what it came from); a link to someone else's post as a bookmark, kept as the post's address rebuilt from its id, never the name and words a shared address carries, and never opened; a link to one of your own posts as a reminder on Today to open that post's analytics at this computer; and a message starting "Draft:" as a draft, as before. The phone's message is never stored as a figure. A short lnkd.in link, which the LinkedIn app's Copy link gives, is kept as it was sent and opened in your own browser when you press Open on Today; Inox Hydra never fetches it, and learns which post it was only from the extension, on the post's page. Until you say which chat is yours, every message is refused; Inox Hydra keeps the chats it refused in memory only, until it stops (each chat's id, how many messages, when, and the first 60 characters of the first), so that its panel can ask whether one is you, and writes only a refused chat's id to its log. Your messages pass through Telegram's servers. The bot token is sealed on your disk like an AI key. Inox Hydra keeps when your chat was paired, so that its panel counts only what came from that chat as seen working, and when it last checked Telegram for your messages: Telegram keeps a bot's messages for one day, so when Inox Hydra checks again more than a day later, the bot sends you, once, a message with three times on your computer's clock (when it last checked, when it checked again, and the moment a day before that) saying that anything sent before then may not have arrived; it never says a message was lost, and never counts any. Both are removed with the bot. To let a reply build an item up and an edit follow it, Inox Hydra keeps which of your messages each item came from, with the words each gave it, and which of the bot's answers were about it, by their numbers in your chat with the bot; a reply to one of them adds its words to that item, with Undo, and is answered with a fixed sentence (the item's first line only with The bot may send me what it writes from my collection on). Those links are counted with your collection, exported and deleted with it. Inox Hydra asks Telegram for the edits you make to your messages too: an edit of a message that made an item replaces its words on the item, never in a draft made from it, and is answered with a fixed sentence. Inox Hydra registers the bot's commands with Telegram as the / menu of your paired chat only: their names and descriptions, its own words and nothing of yours, sent once and again only when they change, and taken away when you pair another chat or remove the bot.

If you configure an AI or image provider, that provider's own privacy policy governs what it does with what you send it. We do not see it, do not proxy it, and hold no key of yours anywhere but on your own disk.

What we do not do

  • We do not sell or transfer your data to anyone. There is no recipient: the data does not reach us.
  • We do not use or transfer your data for any purpose unrelated to the extension's single purpose.
  • We do not use or transfer your data to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes.
  • We do not serve advertising, build advertising profiles, or include any third-party tracker, analytics library or tag.
  • We do not load or execute any code fetched at runtime. Everything the extension runs is in the uploaded package. To notice an update, it reads its own manifest file from its own folder every five minutes, and when the version there differs from the one it runs, it reloads itself from that folder.

Children

Inox Hydra Bridge is a tool for people who use LinkedIn professionally and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If what the extension reads or where anything is sent changes, this page is updated and the "Last updated" date above changes with it. The version of the extension a change applies to is named.
