# What to do today

Open **Today** to see who is waiting on a reply, how your latest post is doing against your usual, and the one LinkedIn page most worth opening now.

Once Setup's required steps are done (the bridge heard from, your profile confirmed and read, and your posts read to the end), the studio opens on **Today**. While the window is in view, Today reads again within about a second of the studio saving a capture from the bridge browser, so captures appear without reloading. It also reads again when you come back to the window, and every two minutes while it is in view, which is how it notices the bridge going quiet. If the studio stopped while Today was open, it comes back by itself once the studio is running again.

## The line under the title

It says whether the bridge browser has been heard from, when anything was last captured (or, before the first capture, how many of your posts the studio holds, for example 12 POSTS OF YOURS HELD), and how many parts of the capture have stopped working. The mark beside **Activity** at the top of the screen says the same about the bridge. If a part has stopped, **Health** shows which one, with each reader's runs in the last 7 days; see [Check the bridge is reading](check-the-bridge-is-reading.md).

## One thing to open

A single suggestion, with its reason. The studio picks it by a fixed order, because each item blocks the ones after it:

| Suggestion | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Go to Setup** | The studio does not know which account is yours, a part of the capture has stopped working, or your post history has not been imported |
| Open LinkedIn | The bridge browser has not been heard from recently |
| Your latest post's analytics page | Your latest post is under three days old and its figures were not read in the last 12 hours |
| A post you asked for from your phone | You shared a link to one of your own posts with your Telegram bot (see [Send a post idea or a post link from your phone with Telegram](send-a-post-idea-from-telegram.md)). The reason reads "You asked from your phone to read this post's analytics", oldest request first. It is done once that post's analytics page has been read after you asked, and closed with a note after a week unread; **Not now** closes it at once. A link to someone else's post never appears here |
| Your latest post | Otherwise, because opening it picks up anyone who has commented since |

**Open it in the bridge browser** opens the page in the bridge browser, where the extension reads it.

## Reply to

Up to five people who commented on your own posts in the last two weeks and have not heard from you. People who only reacted are left off, because there is nothing to reply to, and so is someone whose lead status is **Outreach Sent**, **Connected** or **Meeting Booked**.

They are ordered by what they did: people who engaged with more than one of your posts first, then the most recent comment. The time beside each person is when the studio first saw that comment, which does not change when you open the post again. The ICP score plays no part. Click a person to open their dossier in **Leads**.

Beside the title, the count of every comment waiting in the same two weeks, for example **12 COMMENTS WAITING, OPEN REPLIES**, opens **Replies**, which lists them all, grouped by post. A comment you mark **Answered** there leaves this list too. See [Answer the people waiting on a reply](answer-the-people-waiting-on-a-reply.md).

## Your latest post

The opening of your most recent post, its age, and the reactions and comments last read on it.

If you marked a post as published in the studio and the bridge has not yet seen it on LinkedIn, a line above names it, for example "You marked "Shipping the rota rewrite today." as published Tue 20 Oct, 04:40 PM". The post takes its place here, with its figures, once the bridge sees it.

When it can be done fairly, the studio compares it with your usual:

- Both are read at about two days old, between 36 and 72 hours. A post read at six hours and one read at six weeks say nothing about each other.
- Your usual is the median of your earlier posts read at that age, up to your last 20.
- At least three earlier posts must have a reading at that age.

The figure reads, for example, "70 vs 35 (2.0x) median of 4". Green is at least 1.25 times your usual, orange is 0.8 times or less, and anything between is no difference your posts can separate, so it is not coloured.

When the comparison cannot be made, the screen says why instead of showing a number: the post is too new, it was not read at about two days old, or too few earlier posts were.

A line under the figures says whether the post is still moving, from its last two readings of impressions on one page, for example "Still moving: +50 impressions since the reading 26 hours ago, over 2 readings on its analytics page, the latest 2 hours ago." The page is the one that has read that figure most often for this post, your post's analytics page on a tie, so a single visit to another page never replaces the series. With one reading it says a second one would tell. When either reading was a figure LinkedIn rounded, such as 12K, the change reads "about", and when the change is no larger than the two readings' rounding, the line says they cannot separate it rather than call it growth. Beside it is what is usual for your posts at that age: the share of their two-week impressions your own posts had reached by then, and how many posts that rests on. Until 20 of your posts were read near that age, it says there are not enough instead.

### Where the figures come from

Each time your posts are imported or re-read, the counts LinkedIn shows on them are kept as a reading, with the post's age at that moment. At most one reading per post per hour is kept.

**Open its analytics** opens the post's own analytics page, which only you can see. The extension reads impressions, members reached, reactions, comments, reposts, saves and sends from it, for your own recorded posts only. Where the page shows them it also reads followers gained from this post, profile viewers from this post, link visits, article views and video views, the share of impressions from inside and outside your network, and the top viewer demographics, which are kept with the day they were read so a later visit adds a point rather than replacing one. A figure LinkedIn abbreviated, such as 12K, is stored as 12,000 and marked rounded, and the post's detail on **Posts** names it. LinkedIn may not show each of those newer figures to every member or on every post, so when one is missing nothing is stored for it, never a 0, and the reader is not marked as having stopped reading. The studio keeps, from its own readings, when each was last read and on how many of your posts' analytics pages it has been missing since; if one never appears, the label may have changed. Only a missing impressions, members reached, reactions, comments, reposts, saves or sends figure marks the reader as having stopped reading the page.

## Queue

The next post scheduled to go out, with its date and time, and the next open slot in your weekly posting schedule. Both times use your computer's clock format.

## From your record, today

Once today's cards from **From your record** are built, a strip under the three columns lists them, one line each: which block it is, what it is and the start of its words. Every line, and **OPEN THE CARDS**, opens the section, where a card can be kept, drafted from or opened on LinkedIn; how many slots are waiting on something is counted beside it. Before today's cards are built the strip is not drawn: Today shows no earlier day's cards, and opening Today builds nothing. It appears by itself when the daily job builds them, the first time the studio runs each day. See [Find what to write from your own record](find-what-to-write-from-your-own-record.md).
