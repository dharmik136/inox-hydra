# See what worked, and where your attention goes

Use **Posts** to see which of your posts did better than your usual and who each one brought, **Patterns** to see which forms have gone with more reactions for you, and **Outbound** to see whose content you engage with and whether they engage back.

All three screens are built from your own imported history. Import your posts, comments and reactions from **Setup** first; see [Set up the studio from your own LinkedIn](set-up-the-studio-from-your-linkedin.md).

## Posts

The line under the title gives your usual: the median reactions, comments and impressions of your posts older than the age at which your posts settle. That age is not a fixed week any more: the screen prints the number of days it is using, measured from your own posts' readings where there are enough of them. If those posts have no figures, as posts imported from LinkedIn's archive do not, the line says how many and asks you to press **Import your history** in **Setup**, so the bridge reads them. With too few settled posts it says there is not enough to know your usual yet, rather than showing one.

The posts are a table, one row each, with columns for the post, its date (for example TUE 20 OCT), reach and response, reactions, comments, impressions and the people it brought. Above it:

- **Search your posts** filters by text.
- A **Settled state** group narrows to **All**, **Settled** or **Still growing**.
- **Sort** offers newest, oldest, most reactions, most impressions and most comments.
- The count reads, for example, `1-20 of 1,417`, with **Previous** and **Next** and PAGE 1 OF 71. Twenty rows are shown at a time.

Each figure on a settled row is followed by how it compares with your usual, for example "55 REACTIONS (1.8x)". Green is at least 1.25 times your usual, orange is 0.8 times or less, and anything between is no difference your posts can separate, so it is not coloured. A post that has not settled yet is marked **STILL GROWING** and not compared, because its numbers are still arriving. Reach and response are given as percentiles against your other posts, for example `p72/p48`, or say the post was not read at a comparable age.

The settled posts furthest above your usual reactions are marked **WORTH REPURPOSING**, and the ones furthest below **UNDERPERFORMED**.

The **People** column counts the people each post brought and names the first three, commenters first and then by name, for example "Asha Rao, Ben Okoro, Cora Lind and 9 more"; it never orders them by score. The line under the title also says whether the age at which a post is called settled was measured from your own posts' readings, with how many, or is the studio's assumption until your readings can say.

A post's detail lists its **Readings**: every reading the studio keeps, oldest first, as a series for each page it was read from (your Activity page, its analytics page, or network reading), with "READ 4 TIMES" and, for each reading, the post's age, the day and its impressions, reactions and comments, "about" beside a figure LinkedIn rounded and "not read" where a page did not show one. When old readings are rolled up (**Settings**, **Storage and privacy**), readings older than the rule's age keep only the first and last of each day and every one at 36 to 72 hours old, and the detail says an older post may have been read more often. Above it, **AT ABOUT TWO DAYS OLD** gives the reading nearest 48 hours, which **Today** compares posts at, with the page and age it was read at; without one it says why: not two days old yet, not read yet while it is (open its analytics page now), or not read while it was.

Click a post to see the people the studio captured engaging with it. Click a name to open their dossier in **Leads**. **CONVERSATIONS** counts the people from that post whose lead status you moved to **Connected** or **Meeting Booked**. If a post shows nobody, open it with **Open the post**; its commenters are read when it opens in the bridge browser. Reactions older than 24 months are kept as counts once the studio folds them (see [Choose what the studio keeps](choose-what-the-studio-keeps.md)), so the detail can name fewer people than the row counts; it says how many more are kept only as a count.

Under the table, **Published here, not yet seen on LinkedIn** lists any post marked published in the studio that the bridge has not read on LinkedIn yet, such as one sent with **Send to LinkedIn**. It has no figures or people until the bridge reads it, from your posts page in the bridge browser, and then it moves into the table. The group is not shown when there are none.

Under a post's figures, its detail also says when the post was first seen marked edited on your Activity tab, and a video's watch time and average watch time from its analytics page, with the day each was read; a video whose watch time was never read says **WATCH TIME NOT READ**.

Under the table, once the bridge has read them, **Your reposts** lists your newest reposts with the day you reposted and the repost's own figures (the original post's author, words and figures are never kept), and **Your articles and newsletters** lists each one with every figure as its own analytics page last showed it, with the window that page named or as its total, and the day it was read. A figure the page did not show reads "not read", never 0.

## Patterns

**Patterns** compares your posts that have a form with those that do not, by median reactions, across your posts whose figures have settled and have reactions recorded. The introduction says how many days settled means for you, and whether that was measured from your own readings or assumed until they can say. Posts with none recorded, such as those from LinkedIn's archive, are left out, and a note under the introduction says how many. Each line gives both medians and both counts, for example "MEDIAN 38 REACTIONS ON 7 POSTS WITH IT, 25 ON 11 WITHOUT".

The forms compared are: a short opening line (60 characters or fewer), an opening that asks a question, an opening that starts with an emoji or symbol, an announcement ("excited", "thrilled", "happy to share", a new position), bold or styled Unicode letters, hashtags, and a post over 1,500 characters. A form is compared only when enough posts have it and enough do not. The introduction prints that number, which is the same one the Composer's Evidence panel uses, so nothing is shown here that it would suppress. The others are listed as not enough posts.

When most posts on one side are from a different year than the other, the line is moved under **Mixed up with when you posted**. Your account and audience were different then, so the difference may be about the time rather than the form.

Each form is compared once, by the studio's contrast engine. The lines are the same comparisons the Composer's Evidence panel is built from, so a line this screen shows as a difference is one the Evidence panel shows too, and a form the engine has too few posts for is listed as not enough posts rather than shown. A form whose posts without it have a median of 0 reactions has no ratio to compare, and is listed under NO RATIO CAN BE MEASURED instead.

Under the comparisons, **How your posts settle** draws the share of a post's final figures it had reached by each age, measured from your own readings. Where an age has too few readings to measure, it says so instead of drawing a line.


**Your topics, compared the same way** takes the words and hashtags that recur across your own posts, up to six of them, and compares each one like a form: your settled posts that mention it against those that do not, by median reactions, with both counts, under the same rules and the same number of posts a side as the forms. A topic with too few posts on either side is listed as not enough posts, and one whose two sides come mostly from different years is moved under **Mixed up with when you posted**. A post that mentions two topics counts for both. It is a keyword match, not a reading of what a post means; the Obsidian export's `Topics/` notes use the same words.

Patterns no longer counts qualified leads by topic. A lead's tier is a guess from their job title, and the gap between topics was smaller than the gap between two posts of one topic, so that table ranked noise. Who each post brought is on **Posts**.

These are differences between your posts, not causes. Treat a large one as something to try.

How steadily you post is on the **Queue**, under **Against your own rhythm**, beside the week it is about; see [Use the Queue to plan your posting times](how-the-queue-works.md).

## Outbound

The line under the title counts your reactions and comments on other people's posts, and how many people and pages they were on.

LinkedIn's archive names the post you reacted to or commented on, but not its author, so what the archive import brings in cannot be ranked by person. The screen says how many reactions and comments that is. Run **Posts you reacted to** and **Comments you left** in **Setup**: the bridge reads the authors and adds them to the same rows.

| Section | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Engaged back, matched by display name** | People you engage with who have also engaged with one of your posts |
| **One-way** | People and pages you have engaged with at least three times who have not engaged with your posts |
| **Most engaged with** | Everyone you engage with, most first, with your reactions, your comments, and on how many of your posts they appeared |

In **Most engaged with**, **FIRST SEEN** is when the studio first saw you engage with that account, which is when it read your Activity page, not necessarily the day you engaged. **WHAT YOU LAST SAID TO THEM** is your last comment, or "Only reactions" with the kind of your last reaction (like, celebrate, support, love, insightful or funny), followed by the opening of the last post of theirs you engaged with.

Click the arrow beside a name to open that account: every post of theirs you engaged with, newest first, with what you did and said; and, where the name is one captured person's, their engagements on your posts, with how many days after each of yours they next engaged with one of your posts. Both sides are when the studio saw them, so the days between are as the studio saw it.

**One-way** is not shown until the studio has read who engaged with at least half of your posts. Until then it says how many it has read. Opening your posts in the bridge browser, for example from **Posts**, is what reads them.

> [!NOTE]
> "Engaged back" is matched by display name, and says so beside the figure and the list. A reaction records the author's name, not their profile. A name more than one person you have captured answers to is not matched at all: its row says how many people have that name instead of counting them together.

## Lead history

In a lead's dossier in **Leads**, each change you make under **Pipeline** is now kept, and listed under the buttons with its date, and with the post it came from when you opened the person from one (in **Replies**, or from **Who engaged** in a post's detail). That history is what lets **Posts** count conversations.
